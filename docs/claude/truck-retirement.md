# Retiring a truck — `trucks.retired_at`, the mirror of `in_service_date`
`in_service_date` gave a truck a **first** billed month; nothing gave it a **last**, so a truck that left the fleet kept accruing insurance/ELD/payment/HVUT/IRP into every open month forever. The obvious workaround — flipping `status` to Inactive — is refused by `truckEditLockBlockers()` check (1) whenever it moves a finalized month's figure, and correctly so: `status` has no time dimension, so it removes the truck from **every** month including the finalized ones it was legitimately billed in. There was therefore no correct way to remove a truck from the fleet at all. Named as a known gap in PR #210 and deliberately skipped in PR #216; built here.

**The fleet rule (owner's decision, 2026-09-30): only `Inactive` or `retired_at` stops a truck's fixed costs.** `Maintenance` and `OOS` keep them, because the insurance, payment and ELD are still owed while a truck is in the shop. One rule, `truckInFleet()` / `truckInFleetSql()` in `lib/investor-payout-basis.js` (any status but `Inactive`), read by every fixed-cost, maintenance-fund and compliance-fee site and by the lease coverage; `retired_at` and the in-service date still bound it through `truckChargedInMonth()`. Until then the fixed-cost set was `status = 'Active'`, so the edit guard refused **any** status change on a truck billed into a closed month, a $0/mo truck included, because it would have restated every month. Now Active ↔ Maintenance ↔ OOS moves no money and is never refused. A change in or out of `Inactive` is priced month by month (the months it brings in at the amounts after the save, the months it takes out at the amounts before it) and refused only when a closed month's figure moves: the truck carries a fixed cost, or it has maintenance/compliance rows booked to a closed month; the 409 names the figure. The owner-change check (2a) is priced the same way and also counts the fee rows, which the investor queries join on `owner_id`. **The investor projections read it too (owner decision, 2026-10-01).** `GET /api/investor`'s per-truck figures (the monthly gross and expenses, the est. annual revenue, and each truck's share of the trailing take-home with its ROI and break-even) counted Active trucks only, so a truck in the shop projected $0 and its share went to the investor's other trucks (on the local copy: `Logisx-#91` $740 → $2,236/mo, ROI 32.3% → 97.6%, break-even 38 → 13 months, with `LogisX-#33` in Maintenance). They now read `truckInFleet()`: Maintenance and OOS keep their share, Inactive still drops out and a retirement still marks a truck too short to project. The ELD feed alerts (`lib/eld-feed-health.js`) stay Active only: they are about a running truck, not money. `scripts/test-truck-retirement.js` §1 pins every site to the rule, §10 runs the guard and §11 the projection shares. ⚠️ **What this can move on existing data:** a truck already in Maintenance or OOS with fixed costs or fee rows now counts them in every month it is billed in, open and closed (a settled payout stays frozen, so a closed month's recompute shows as drift, and through the loss carry-forward it can move an open month). Check production for such a truck before this ships.

- **⚠️ INCLUSIVE, and that is what makes the feature usable.** The retirement month IS billed, in full, no proration — a truck in service 2026-04-15 is billed all of April, so a truck retired 2026-07-27 is billed all of July. The interval a human reads off the two date fields is `[in_service_date, retired_at]`, both ends inclusive. Under the *exclusive* reading (`retired_at` = first unbilled month), retiring a truck at the end of the month that just closed would REMOVE that month's costs from a finalized period and be refused — i.e. the feature would be unusable for the exact case it exists for. **A truck retired mid-month bills that whole month.**
- **⚠️ THE CLIENT HAS NOT SIGNED OFF ON THE PRORATION QUESTION.** The convention above is derived from `in_service_date`'s own behaviour and is internally consistent, but "is the retirement month billed in full?" is an accounting decision, and insurance genuinely can be cancelled mid-month with a refund. The two answers differ by one month × up to **$3,105.83** on a single truck. Confirm before anyone retires a truck in anger.
  - **✅ Verified live 2026-09-19: `retired_at` is EMPTY on all six trucks, so this question has ZERO live exposure today.** Nothing is retired, the predicate bills nothing differently, and no locked month is affected. It remains **open and unanswered** — this note changes nothing about the rule, it only records that there is time to decide properly rather than under pressure.
  - **$3,105.83 is a CEILING, not a loss.** It is `Logisx-#91`'s full monthly fixed cost (insurance 1,680 + payment 1,210 + IRP 117.50 + ELD 50 + HVUT 48.33), and that truck entered service 2026-08-04 so it carries no locked-month exposure at all. The realistic case is `LogisX-#302` at $1,520/mo, where whole-month vs prorated differs by **$196.13** — about $98 of it the investor's half.
  - ⚠️ **If the answer ever becomes "prorate", the retroactive-edit guard does NOT cover it.** That guard compares the symmetric difference of the billed-month SET; under proration the retirement month is still *in* the set, just worth less, so `moved` is empty, nothing blocks, and a closed month's total silently drops behind a 200 response. The guard is month-shaped; proration is dollar-shaped.
- **One predicate, every gate.** `truckChargedInMonth(t, monthKey)` is the single both-bounds-inclusive test; `truckChargeUntilMonth()` is the upper-bound companion to `truckChargeFromMonth()`. Every money month-gate **and** `truckFixedCostLockedMonths()` (the guard) call it, so the guard cannot disagree with the math it protects. That is the whole point — PR #216 correctly identified "a mirrored upper bound, *or the guard disagrees with the math it protects*" as the hard part, and the answer was to stop mirroring and share. (There were four money gates when this was written, and there are more now; `scripts/test-truck-retirement.js` §1 holds the current count, lists the gates and names the newest, `GET /api/investor/report`'s, added 2026-09-30.)
- **Two shapes, not one.** Clamping the month gate does nothing to a multiplication, so the **all-time totals** need their own end bound: `truckBilledMonthCount(t, now)` replaced four hand-rolled copies of `(now − start) + 1`. A fifth site, `truckMonthsInPeriod()` in `GET /api/investor/report`, re-derived the start with **day** granularity and was invisible to a grep for `truckChargeFromMonth(`, so it was clamped separately. **That site is why `retired_at` is a DATE, not a bare month**: it clamped against a caller-supplied date range, and a month-only column would have forced an invented "last day of the month" there. **It was removed on 2026-09-30.** It charged fixed costs in months the payouts ledger never charges (idle months, and months before the investor's first load). The report now walks the months the ledger charged and gates each truck with `truckChargedInMonth()`, a sixth month gate. See [`investor-money-math.md`](investor-money-math.md). The column stays a DATE; nothing reads it with day granularity any more.
- **⚠️ `""` MEANS UNBOUNDED AT BOTH ENDS.** `truckChargeFromMonth()` returns `""` for "no usable in-service date" and the money math reads it as **charge every month**; `truckChargeUntilMonth()` returns `""` for "not retired" and the math reads it as **charge forever**. A guard reading either `""` the other way waves through precisely the truck with the most exposure — the inversion corrected in PR #205 and again in PR #216. The upper-bound version is strictly more dangerous because `""` is the **default value of every existing row**, so misreading it would report zero exposure for the entire fleet.
- **⚠️ Every fixed-cost `SELECT` list must carry `retired_at`.** Eight of them name columns explicitly; an absent column makes `truckChargeUntilMonth()` return `""` and the bound silently never fires. Asserted textually by the harness.
- **⚠️ `truckChargeUntilMonth()` range-checks the month and `truckChargeFromMonth()` does not — deliberately.** `'2026-13-01'` passes a bare shape test and slices to `'2026-13'`, which every real 2026 month sorts *below*, so a truck "retired" that way would bill all of 2026 and then silently stop in 2027. The FROM half is left alone because it has a `created_at` **fallback**, so tightening it would change which months an existing malformed row bills — a live behaviour change on rows that already have money against them.
- **Setting `retired_at` is itself retroactive**, so it gets guard check **(4b)**, a clone of the `in_service_date` check: the months at risk are the **symmetric difference** of billed-before and billed-after, so backdating into a finalized month is 409 `PERIOD_FINALIZED` (naming the field, the months and the dollars) while retiring effective the current or a future month is 200. Clearing the date (un-retiring) *adds* months back and is caught by the same difference with no extra branch. The dollars both date checks name price a month the date brings **in** at the amounts **after** the save and a month it takes **out** at the amounts **before** it, because the Trucks form sends the costs and the dates in one save: until 2026-09-30 both priced at the stored amounts, so setting insurance to $1,000/mo while backdating the in-service date was refused as adding "$0.00" (`scripts/test-truck-retirement.js` §9). 400 `RETIRED_BEFORE_IN_SERVICE` guards the cross-field case — an empty interval would make the truck behave like one that never existed.
- **`POST /api/trucks` deliberately does NOT accept it** (a truck is not created retired), which removes a whole gate from `truckCreateLockBlockers`.
- **Scope: `retired_at` is a BILLING bound, not a soft delete.** This is exactly why PR #210's objection to `trucks.deleted_at` ("nine-plus query sites, and missing one means a deleted truck keeps billing") does not apply: a retired truck stays visible everywhere — trucks list, `truckCount`, `getInvestorDriverSet`, driver-pay rate maps — and only its **fixed-cost accrual** stops. Deliberately **not** changed: the ten `NOT IN` subqueries that scope maintenance/compliance fees by status (since 2026-09-30 they read the fleet rule, `NOT (truckInFleetSql())`; those rows are **dated**, so they stop naturally; excluding them would retroactively remove real costs from closed months — the exact harm this feature prevents), and `/api/financials`' per-truck month count for a truck **with loads**, which is bounded by its own load span already.
- **Fixed at the same time**: `perTruckData`'s `avgMonthlyGross`/`avgMonthlyExpenses` divided by `truckMonths` whose floor is **0** — a live `Math.round(x/0)` → `Infinity` → `null` in JSON. Reachable before (a forward-dated truck) but ordinary once a truck can retire.
- **Measured on production, read-only, 2026-08-09.** 6 trucks, all Active, 15 locked periods (2025-05…2026-07); only 2026-08 open; two payouts already **PAID**. Fixed costs currently billed into locked months: `LogisX-#33` **$12,173.32**, `LogisX-#2372` **$7,487.32**, `LogisX-#302` **$6,080.00**, the other three $0. Fleet rate **$9,540.99/mo**. `LogisX-#302` last pinged 2026-07-27 — the live retirement candidate, and under the inclusive rule `retired_at = 2026-07-27` is **allowed** (July stays billed) while `2026-06-30` is refused.
- Verified: **129/129** unit assertions against source **extracted** from `server.js` (`node scripts/test-truck-retirement.js`) — including a deliberately independent oracle, since testing the predicate against itself proves nothing — plus **61/61** end-to-end HTTP on a scratch DB. Migration is additive, idempotent (`ALTER` in try/catch) and reversible (`UPDATE trucks SET retired_at = ''` restores every figure exactly).
